# Reading the standardized score reports and the FBA tools

How to read the two instruments the payers require, and the tools used to find each behavior's function. Report numbers exactly as the score report prints them; this file only explains what they mean.

## Vineland-3

- The Adaptive Behavior Composite and the domain standard scores (Communication, Daily Living Skills, Socialization, and Motor Skills for younger children) have a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15. Subdomain v-scale scores have a mean of 15 and a standard deviation of 3.
- Lower adaptive scores mean more impairment.
- On the Maladaptive Behavior domain the direction reverses: higher v-scale scores (Internalizing, Externalizing, Maladaptive Behavior Index) mean more problem behavior.

## BASC-3 Parenting Relationship Questionnaire (PRQ)

- T-scores have a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10.
- Scales: Attachment, Communication, Discipline Practices, Involvement, Parenting Confidence and Relational Frustration; the Child/Adolescent form (ages 6 to 18) adds Satisfaction with School, which the Preschool form does not have.
- Low scores on Attachment, Communication, Discipline Practices, Involvement, Parenting Confidence and Satisfaction with School are the areas of concern; on Relational Frustration, high scores are.

## Levels

Use the adaptive levels and classifications printed on each report (Low, Moderately Low, Elevated, At-Risk, Clinically Significant and so on). Do not assign a level the report does not give.

## FBA tools: indirect and direct evidence

The Vineland-3 and the BASC-3 PRQ describe global functioning; they never establish a behavior's function. Function comes from the tools below. Indirect tools (interviews and rating scales filled in by a caregiver or teacher) report what an informant believes; direct tools (the BCBA's ABC recording, scatterplots, observation notes) record what was seen. Name the kind of evidence in the sentence.

### FAST (Functional Analysis Screening Tool)

- An indirect screening questionnaire completed with an informant, one behavior at a time. Its items are grouped by function: social reinforcement by attention or preferred items, social reinforcement by escape from demands, automatic reinforcement by sensory stimulation, and automatic reinforcement by pain attenuation.
- The result is the number of items endorsed in each group, out of the group's total (for example "escape 4 of 4"). The group with the most endorsements points to the likely function; groups that tie, or several high groups, point to more than one.
- It is a screening: write that it "suggests" or "supports" a function, never that it shows one. Report the counts exactly as the form gives them.

### MAS (Motivation Assessment Scale)

- An indirect rating scale completed with an informant for one behavior, with items rated 0 to 6 across four functions: sensory, escape, attention and tangible.
- The result is a mean item score per function; the highest mean is the function the informant's ratings rank first. Report the means as given (for example "escape 4.5, tangible 3.8") and do not compute ones the form does not give.
- Like the FAST, it supports a hypothesis; it does not confirm one.

### ABC recording and observation notes

- Direct recording of each episode: the antecedent (what happened just before), the behavior (what the child did, in observable terms), and the consequence (what happened just after, especially what the adults did and what the child got or avoided).
- What it gives: the antecedents and consequences that the payers require for each behavior, this child's actual topography (the source of the behavior's definition and its examples), and counts and durations per observation.
- A consequence that repeats across episodes (the task removed, the tablet given, the adult's attention) is evidence for the function it would maintain.

### Scatterplots

- Direct recording of whether, or how often, a behavior occurred in each time block across days.
- What it gives: when the behavior is more and less likely, which points to the routines and setting events around it. It does not give the function by itself, and when it records only occurrence, it gives no counts per block.

### ABLLS-R (Assessment of Basic Language and Learning Skills, Revised)

- A criterion-referenced skills assessment, not a norm-referenced test: it has no standard scores. It scores many skills across lettered domains (for example cooperation and reinforcer effectiveness, visual performance, receptive language, imitation, requests, labeling, intraverbal, social interaction, self-help), each skill by the highest criterion the child meets.
- What it gives: which skills the child has and which are missing, as targets for acquisition and replacement goals. Report skills and domains as the report names them; do not turn its results into a level or compare them with same-age peers.

### Preference assessments

- Ways of finding what the child currently prefers, which tells the plan what can work as a reinforcer: caregiver interview or checklist (indirect), free-operant observation (time spent with items when all are available), and trial-based methods (single stimulus, paired stimulus, multiple stimulus with or without replacement), which give a ranking.
- Report the items and the ranking or result as recorded, and the method only when the sources name it. A preference is not proof that an item reinforces behavior; preferences change, so the plan reassesses them.

## Combining evidence into a hypothesis

- The function stated in an assessment is a hypothesis. Write "hypothesized function" or "the data suggest ... maintained by ...", never "the function is".
- The hypothesis is strongest when the indirect tools and direct observation agree. Name each kind of evidence: the FAST or MAS result, the caregiver interview, and what the BCBA observed after the behavior.
- When they disagree, report both and attribute each; do not choose for her. When she states the function in her list of behaviors or in her sections, that is the hypothesis to use.
- A primary and a secondary function are stated only when the sources give them.
- One topography can serve more than one function, and different topographies can share one. Group behaviors by function only as she does.
- Self-injury or another behavior with no social pattern in the data may be automatically maintained; say so only when the sources suggest it (for example the FAST's sensory items, or episodes when the child is alone).
