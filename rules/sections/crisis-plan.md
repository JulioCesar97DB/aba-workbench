# Section 20: Crisis Plan

## What this section is for

In the agency's assessments this section holds the protocol the team and the caregivers follow when a behavior puts the child or others at risk. In ABA Matrix it has a child-specific narrative first, then template blocks (risk factors to check, what caregivers must do to prevent a crisis, questions on risk of harm to self or others, and techniques for ending a crisis), then a monitoring part she writes. Write only the two narrative parts; the blocks are forms.

## The child-specific part

In her assessments it scales with the risk the assessment found:

- For a child with a high-risk behavior: a step-by-step protocol for that behavior.
- For a child whose behaviors interfere but do not endanger anyone: a plain statement that the child does not need a crisis protocol for any one behavior, the ordinary responses to the behaviors, and one safety situation with the supervision it needs and when emergency services are called.
- For a child with several dangerous behaviors: a paragraph on scope (which behaviors the plan covers, that teaching stops during a crisis, how close the adult stays, when emergency services or poison control are called, who is notified), a paragraph for each dangerous behavior, and what follows a crisis.

Which of these fits, and every step, distance and threshold in it, is hers to decide (see the three kinds of content). Write what the sources give for this child: the dangerous behaviors as the assessment names them, the times, triggers and early signs the sources describe, and the family's current responses, described plainly and without blame. Give a protocol step, a supervision distance or the point at which emergency services are called only as her standing instructions, her sections or the sources give it. Where the plan needs one that they do not give, keep the sentence and mark it [MISSING: ...]. Do not supply de-escalation techniques of your own. Write the steps of a protocol as sentences in order, not as a numbered list (the format for pasting into ABA Matrix allows no lists).

Never propose seclusion or manual, mechanical or chemical restraint: they are not covered services, and nothing in the sources authorizes them. When her instructions give a protocol, it says that no physical restraint or restrictive procedure is used.

## The monitoring part

How the plan is monitored, as her assessments describe it: data collected on each behavior as it is defined; a weekly review of the data and graphs by the BCBA; observation of the RBT's and the caregivers' fidelity to the plan; whether the caregivers find the plan workable at home; how the plan is adjusted if progress stalls; and how incidents are documented and the plan revised after one.

## Not yet settled with the BCBA

Not settled: her standard protocol wording and the agency's requirements for caregivers during sessions (her standing instructions for this section are the place for them); how and how fast incidents are reported; and whether she wants protocol steps numbered when ABA Matrix's field accepts it.
