# Section 7: Environmental Changes

From the case map, this section gets in full the facts whose home is section 7. Everything else comes by name only (the caregivers by relation, the settings, the other services, the behaviors), so the names stay the same; the examples come from the sources.

## What this section is for

It tells the reviewer and the team how this child copes with change, which matters for every transition the plan asks of the child, what changes in the child's environment are happening or coming, and the supports that will be used.

The changes that count, as she chose them: a change of school or grade, a change of who cares for the child, a new sibling, a change of medication, a change of RBT or BCBA. A move or a change of the family's routine is written too when the sources report one.

## How to write it

Prose, 2 or 3 paragraphs: about 300 to 450 words when she gives the supports, shorter when they are still [MISSING].

1. How the child responds to changes in place, routine and people, with examples from the sources, each attributed (who reported it or in which observation it was seen, and what the child did). Then a sentence of your own saying what the examples show together.
2. The changes the sources report, each attributed: who reported it, when it happened or will happen (a month and year, or the day written "December 15, 2026" when the exact start matters), and how the child has responded so far.
3. The supports that will be used. She always writes this paragraph, even when no change is coming: the child still meets everyday changes, and the plan says how they are supported. In her assessments the supports are concrete (a session routine the child can predict, advance notice of each change, visual supports kept up to date when plans change, practice that starts with small changes, prompts to request help or extra time, and cues kept the same across settings). The list above only describes her assessments. Write none of these, or any other support, unless the sources, her sections, her standing instructions or her notes give it: supports are her standard practice or her choice for this child (see the four kinds of content). When none are given, write the paragraph's opening and mark the rest once: [MISSING: supports for changes in routine, place and people, and for the changes above].

If the sources report no coming change, say so in one sentence, naming who was asked when the sources say so; the child's response to everyday changes and the supports are still written.
