# Section 18: Discharge and Service Fading Plan

## What this section is for

It tells the reviewer when and how services will end: the objective criteria for discharge and how hours will be faded as the child progresses. Molina returns plans whose criteria are not objective and individualized; "when clinically appropriate" is not a criterion.

## What to write

The narrative only, in 2 or 3 paragraphs. In ABA Matrix this section also has a table of three phases; do not write the table, and do not describe the phases in the narrative either.

Her assessments build the narrative in this order:

1. The grounds for discharge. State the payer's (see the payer facts, Molina section 8: the child no longer meets medical necessity; no longer engages in maladaptive behaviors; the behaviors no longer stand in the way of major life activities; the impairment no longer justifies services; the parent or guardian withdraws consent) and any others her standing instructions give. This sentence names the grounds only: it is always followed by the measurable criteria described under "Criteria, not commentary".
2. When fading begins, and what happens if skills decline while it is under way.
3. When services end after fading.

What starts fading, what happens if skills slip, and the periods in 2 and 3 are her standard practice (see the four kinds of content): write them as her standing instructions for this section give them. When they do not, keep each sentence and mark what is missing [MISSING: ...].

Within that order:

- Discharge criteria tied to this child: the behaviors and skills named in the assessment, with measurable conditions drawn from the plan (for example a reduction of the named behavior, or a skill performed across the settings named), and the caregivers' ability to carry out the strategies. Do not invent target numbers: a criterion whose number is not in the sources or her sections gets [MISSING: ...].
- How services will be faded: hours reduced gradually as goals are met, caregiver training continuing as direct hours decrease and, as the payer expects of a strong transition plan, becoming more frequent as the child nears the discharge criteria, and the transition planned with the family and other providers named in the sources.
- The community resources and supports that will help keep the gains after discharge, only as the sources or her instructions name them; otherwise [MISSING: community resources and supports after discharge].
- Circumstances that lead to discharge or transition other than meeting goals, only when the sources or her instructions give them.

Do not state hours or units for the current request; they belong to Summary and Recommendations.

## Criteria, not commentary

- Each criterion has the form the payer expects: the behavior or skill as the assessment names it, a measurable level, and how long it must hold (for example: a named behavior at a stated rate or lower for a stated number of weeks, against its baseline). The level and the period come from the sources or her sections and instructions; when they do not, the sentence keeps its form with [MISSING: ...] where the number goes. A threshold is her choice (see the four kinds of content).
- "When clinically appropriate", "when goals are met" or "when progress is sufficient" can name a ground for discharge, but never stand in for a criterion's measurable part: a criterion always says which behavior or skill, what level and for how long.
- No sentences that comment on the evidence or on how decisions will be made in general, such as "These are school goals, not evidence that she has met them" or "Decisions will depend on whether gains are maintained, not on the dates of those changes". State the criterion or the step; leave the reasoning to the reviewer.
