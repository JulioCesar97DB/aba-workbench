# Section 19: Risk Assessment

From the case map, this section uses the risks; the behaviors (names, incidents and intensity). The other parts only keep names and numbers the same.

## What this section is for

It states the risks the assessment found, to the child, to others and to the success of the plan, so the team and the reviewer see what the plan and the crisis plan protect against. The payer facts list safety among the impairments that qualify for services: aggression, self-injury, property destruction, elopement, and difficulty recognizing risk or danger.

## What to write

Prose, 3 or 4 paragraphs and about 300 to 400 words, with no risk rating. In her assessments the order is:

1. Risks to the plan itself: what could keep it from working for this child, as the sources point to it (for example cues that differ from one caregiver or setting to another, or a child who waits for prompts), stated as reasoning from the facts.
2. The risks from the behaviors, in prose, starting with the most dangerous and ending with those that interfere only with learning or participation: each behavior as the assessment names it, how often and how intensely it occurs, where and with whom, who is exposed, and any injury or near miss reported, each attributed. Risks from the child's difficulty recognizing danger (streets, water, strangers, objects) only as the sources report them. Then the non-behavioral risks the sources report: pica, seizures, allergies, water (pools, canals), a very limited diet, medication side effects. Leave out one the sources do not report.
3. What the family does today when a crisis happens, as the caregivers report it, written in ABA terms (moving a sibling away is "removing others from the area", putting breakable things away is "environmental manipulation", waiting until the child is calm is "waiting for the behavior to end"), plainly and without blame. Say that the plan keeps the family's own strategies wherever it can: this is her standard practice, confirmed by her questionnaire answers of 2026-11-27, so write it as hers, not as a proposal. Then how the plan answers the risks: with least-restrictive, function-based procedures, naming only those the sources or her sections give, and referring the reader to the Crisis Plan for the dangerous behaviors.
4. How the risk to the plan is kept small. Refer to caregiver training by name only, as part of the answer: its method, format, language and content belong to Family/Caregiver Involvement, so never restate them here (no "Behavioral Skills Training", "in person", languages or caregivers trained). Name no procedure in this paragraph. How implementation fidelity is checked and how the plan is adjusted are her standard practice (see the four kinds of content): write them as her standing instructions for this section give them; otherwise keep the sentence and mark it [MISSING: how implementation fidelity is checked and how the plan is adjusted].

When the sources report no risk in an area the payer facts list, do not state that the risk is absent unless a source says so.

## What it leaves to other sections

This section states risks, their evidence and the family's current crisis responses. The safety measures the family has already taken (knives out of reach, no firearms, locks, alarms) belong to the Crisis Plan; name one here only in a clause, when it bears on the size of a risk. The protocol (what staff and caregivers do during an episode from now on, when emergency services are called) belongs to the Crisis Plan. Changes in the child's environment belong to Environmental Changes. The everyday responses that caregiver training will change, and how that training is given, belong to Family/Caregiver Involvement.
