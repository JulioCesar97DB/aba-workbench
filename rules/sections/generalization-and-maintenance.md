# Section 17: Generalization and Maintenance

## What this section is for

It tells the reviewer and the team how the skills taught in sessions will carry over to other settings and people, and how they will be kept once learned. The BCBA has given its structure herself; follow it exactly.

## Required structure

Exactly two paragraphs.

1. Generalization. Skills and replacement behaviors are taught with the same SD (discriminative stimulus) and then practiced across settings and people: name this child's settings (home, school, community, as the assessment names them) and people (the caregivers, siblings, teachers, RBTs the sources name). The prompt hierarchy is adjusted as the child's proficiency grows. Use examples taken from this child's own goals and replacement behaviors, as named elsewhere in the assessment.

2. Maintenance. Weekly probes check that each mastered skill is still performed. The reinforcement schedule moves from continuous to intermittent, and prompts are faded. Close by saying that if performance drops, the reinforcement schedule and the prompts that were removed are reinstated, and removed again once the level is regained.

## Gaps in this section

The two paragraphs need this child's goals and settings. If the sources and her other sections do not name any goals yet, keep the structure and mark the examples [MISSING: the child's goals, from the behavior plan].
