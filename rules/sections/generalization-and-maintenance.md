# Section 17: Generalization and Maintenance

From the case map, this section gets in full the facts whose home is section 17. Everything else comes by name only (the caregivers by relation, the settings, the behaviors), so the people, settings and behaviors it names stay the same.

## What this section is for

It tells the reviewer and the team how the skills taught in sessions will carry over to other settings and people, and how they will be kept once learned. The BCBA has given its structure herself; follow it exactly.

## Required structure

Exactly two paragraphs.

It is written for the whole plan, not goal by goal (her answer: the general plan is enough), in about 150 to 300 words.

1. Generalization. Skills and replacement behaviors are taught with the same discriminative stimulus (SD) and then practiced across settings and people: name this child's settings (home, school, community, as the assessment names them) and people (the caregivers, siblings, teachers, RBTs the sources name). The prompt hierarchy is adjusted as the child's proficiency grows. One or two brief examples taken from this child's own goals and replacement behaviors, as named elsewhere in the assessment, make it this child's.

2. Maintenance. After mastery, weekly probes check that each skill is still performed. The reinforcement schedule is thinned from continuous to intermittent, and prompts are faded. Close by saying that if performance drops, the reinforcement schedule and the prompts that were removed are reinstated, and removed again once the level is regained.

This is her own description of the section. Anything more that she writes the same way in every case (for example how she programs for generalization beyond settings and people) is her standard practice: add it only as her standing instructions for this section give it (see the four kinds of content). Care coordination with other providers has its own field in ABA Matrix and is not written here.
