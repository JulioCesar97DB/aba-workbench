# Section 16: Family/Caregiver Involvement

## What this section is for

It shows the reviewer that the caregivers will take part in treatment and how: who will participate, what they will learn, and why that training matters for this child. Caregiver participation is something the payers look for, and when a parent cannot participate the assessment has to say why, what that means for treatment and how it is mitigated (see the payer facts).

## What to write

The narrative only: her general observations about the family's involvement. In ABA Matrix this section also holds caregiver goals (safety protocol, interventions and environmental manipulations, replacement and acquisition goals, use of reinforcement), each with its own ladder of objectives. ABA Matrix builds those ladders itself from the measurement unit, the period and the accuracy the BCBA enters, so never write objectives, ladders, start dates or mastery criteria here.

1. Who the caregivers are and who will participate in caregiver training, with their availability as the sources report it (work schedules, languages, other children at home), how involved they are now, and the family's priorities as Background Information gives them.
2. The caregivers' everyday responses that training will change (for example giving the item back to end an outburst, or doing a step for the child), as the sources report them, plainly and without blame. This is where training starts. What the family does during a dangerous episode (blocking, carrying the child away, calling for help) belongs to the Crisis Plan.
3. What caregiver training will cover, tied to this child: the safety protocol for the behaviors argued earlier, the antecedent strategies and environmental changes the plan uses, prompting and reinforcing the replacement and acquisition skills, and using reinforcement consistently. Name the behaviors, skills and procedures exactly as the rest of the assessment names them, and say in one clause what the caregiver will do with each, in which routine. Do not describe the procedures step by step: their steps, timing and prompts belong to the Interventions section. Name only procedures that the sources, her sections, instructions or notes give; choosing them is hers (see the four kinds of content).
4. Why: what the parent interview and the BASC-3 PRQ show about the caregivers' needs (for example parenting confidence or relational frustration), stated as the report gives it.
5. When a caregiver cannot participate, or participation is limited: the reason as reported, the likely effect on treatment, and the plan to reduce it. If the sources do not give the reason or the plan, mark it [MISSING: ...].

Do not state hours, units or service codes; they belong to Summary and Recommendations.

How training will be delivered (the method, who trains whom, when, in what language) is her standard practice or her choice for this child (see the four kinds of content). Write it only as her standing instructions for this section, her sections, her notes or the sources give it; otherwise leave it out, except where participation is limited and the payer needs the plan to reduce it (point 5), where it is marked [MISSING: ...].

## Length

As in her assessments: 1 to 4 paragraphs, about 200 to 400 words. The five points above can share paragraphs. Density over coverage: who, what and why, not how each procedure works.
