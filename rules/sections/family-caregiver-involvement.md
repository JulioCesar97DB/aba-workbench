# Section 16: Family/Caregiver Involvement

## What this section is for

It shows the reviewer that the caregivers will take part in treatment and how: who will participate, how and where they will be trained, what they will learn, and why that training matters for this child. Caregiver participation is something the payers look for, and when a parent cannot participate the assessment has to say why, what that means for treatment and how it is mitigated (see the payer facts).

## What to write

The narrative only: her general observations about the family's involvement. In ABA Matrix this section also holds the caregiver goals, one set for all the caregivers who take part, each with its own ladder of objectives (usually three: interventions and environmental manipulations, replacement and acquisition goals, use of reinforcement; a fourth for a safety plan the caregivers carry out, or for a skill that only applies at home, such as a toileting goal). ABA Matrix builds the ladders itself, so never write objectives, ladders, start dates or mastery criteria here, and do not list the goals in the narrative.

Refer to the caregivers as "the caregiver" or "the caregivers", or by relation ("her mother", "her grandmother"); never by name.

Her answer gives what this section says: who takes part, the method, where, what is trained and why. Nothing more.

1. Who takes part: the caregivers who will participate, with their work hours and availability and how involved they are now, as the sources report them. This section owns these facts. Background Information owns the household: who lives in the home, who works, the languages spoken at home and a parent who lives elsewhere. Keep every fact that bears on participation: a caregiver who is absent or has no regular contact, referred to in a clause because Background Information states it ("with her father not involved"), and a caregiver's stress, fatigue or other limits, as the sources give them. When participation is limited, give the reason as reported, its likely effect on treatment and the plan to reduce it; if the sources give no reason or plan, mark it [MISSING: ...]. Refer to the family's priorities in a clause ("the priorities the family named"), never in Background's words.
2. How and where: training uses Behavioral Skills Training (BST), in person, in the child's home or the setting where services are given, in the caregivers' language. It is never given by telehealth. This is her confirmed practice; write it as hers.
3. What is trained and why: the plan's strategies for the behaviors argued earlier, the replacement and acquisition skills and the use of reinforcement, by the names the plan gives them once it exists, and why this child needs the caregivers to carry them out (the everyday responses the sources report, such as giving the item back to end an outburst, described plainly and without blame). Name no procedure, routine or starting point for training that her plan, her standing instructions, her notes or a source does not name: until her plan is written, say what areas training covers, not which procedure the caregiver will use with which skill, or where training will start. What the family does during a crisis belongs to Risk Assessment. The BASC-3 PRQ belongs to Medical Necessity; mention it here only when a result bears directly on what training covers, in words, without scores.

Do not state hours, units or service codes; they belong to Summary and Recommendations.

## Length

As in her assessments: 1 to 4 paragraphs, about 200 to 400 words. Density over coverage: who, how, where, what and why, not how each procedure works.
