# Section 3: Background Information

## What this section is for

It introduces the child to a reader who has never met them: who the child is, who they live with, how they came to be diagnosed, what has happened in their development and health, where they go to school, what help they already receive, and what brought the family to ABA. Every later section leans on it: the reviewer reads Medical Necessity, the behaviors and the plan against this picture, so names, dates, people and services set here must be the ones the rest of the assessment uses.

Weak versions read like a form filled in sentence by sentence, could describe any child with the same diagnosis, leave out who reported each fact, or slip in history the sources do not give.

## What it covers

In the agency's assessments this section is a narrative that covers, as far as the sources give them:

1. The child and the family: age, who the child lives with, siblings, the languages spoken at home and the child's own language use.
2. The diagnosis as the CDE states it: the diagnosis, who made it and when. Current medication, as reported.
3. Developmental and medical history: pregnancy and birth when reported, milestones, medical conditions, hospitalizations, sleep, feeding and toileting when the sources mention them.
4. School: the program or grade, the schedule, and the school's plan (IEP or ESE plan) when there is one.
5. Other therapies and prior services: speech therapy, occupational therapy, earlier ABA, with frequency and setting as reported. These belong here, not in another section. Speech or occupational therapy the school gives under the child's ESE plan is described as part of the school's plan, not as a separate service the family receives, because it is not a paid service.
6. The behaviors and concerns that led to this assessment, briefly and in the caregivers' and evaluator's terms. They are defined and measured in later sections; here they explain why the family is seeking ABA.

Cite the source inside the sentence: the CDE, the IEP, the parent interview, the FBA questionnaires. Write it as connected paragraphs, not as one sentence per item.

## What it owns, and what it leaves to other sections

This section owns the child's history and circumstances: the referral and who made it, the family and the languages, the diagnosis as the CDE states it (who and when), the medications, the developmental and medical history, the school and its plan, and every other service the child receives. Other sections refer to these facts; they do not retell them, so state them fully and once here.

It does not own the behaviors, the scores or the current changes:

- The behaviors and concerns that led to the referral are named in one or two sentences, in the caregivers' and the evaluator's terms. Their counts, incidents, functions and risks belong to Medical Necessity, the behavior sections and Risk Assessment. An incident is told here only when it is the reason for the referral, and then briefly.
- Vineland-3 and BASC-3 PRQ results belong to Medical Necessity. The results of the tests the CDE used to diagnose (for example the ADOS-2 or an intelligence test) are given here, briefly and as the CDE reports them, next to the diagnosis, since no other section owns them; whether she wants them here is pending her answer.
- Changes happening now or coming (a new school, a move, a change of caregiver) belong to Environmental Changes. Mention a change here only when it is part of the child's history.

## Gaps in this section

- Leave out a topic the sources say nothing about, unless the payer facts make it expected (the diagnosis and who made it, other services the child receives); then mark it [MISSING: ...].
- If two sources give a different date, name or service, attribute each or mark it [CHECK: ...].
