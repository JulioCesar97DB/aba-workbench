# Programs: replacements, skills and caregiver goals

For the Plan step: the replacement behaviors and the skills acquisition programs (sections 9, 11 and 12) and the caregiver goals of Family/Caregiver Involvement (section 16). No request loads this file yet; the Plan step will, with the interventions rules. These are her defaults: a standing instruction, or a note of hers for this case, overrides them. ABA Matrix builds the goal ladders (the STOs and the LTO) from what she enters, so never write the ladders.

## Replacements

- Every function of every target behavior has at least one functionally equivalent replacement: a response that gets the child the same outcome the behavior gets. Escape: asking for a break or for help. Tangibles: asking for the item, or waiting for it. Attention: asking for attention in an appropriate way. Sensory and automatic reinforcement: an activity that competes with the behavior.
- The replacement is accepted in any form the child can use: speech, a sign, a gesture or a picture exchange.
- Replacements count as programs (below).

## How many programs

Programs are the replacements and the skills acquisition goals together.

- A child under 5: about 25 programs.
- A child of 5 or older: about one program for each weekly hour of direct therapy; her usual plan is 30 hours a week, so about 30. The hours are hers to set: when she has not given them, plan for about 30 and add an open item that says so.
- Never pad the list: every program answers a need the sources show. When the sources do not support that many, propose fewer and say why.

## Which skills

- Choose from the behavior cusps for the child's age, and from what the child needs to function as independently as children of that age do.
- Draw on, and name for each skill: low items of the Vineland-3, the ABLLS-R (only for a child under 5), the VB-MAPP, the family's priorities and her direct observation.
- Usual areas: communication (mands, tacts, intraverbals), daily living and self-care, toileting, safety, social and play skills, waiting and tolerance, community skills.

## How a program is written

- The definition says what the child does when the opportunity comes, in observable terms, with its scoring rule.
- Every phrase the child is taught or prompted to say is written in English ("help, please", "break"), whatever language the family speaks at home. The family's language for therapy belongs only in the first paragraph of Background Information.
- A skill with a task analysis has its steps numbered inside the procedure, as few as possible: 4 to 6 steps. No step is a physiological need: a toileting task analysis never counts the voiding itself as a step.

## Baselines

- A skill's baseline comes from 10 opportunities, so it is a multiple of 10% ("A baseline of 30%.").
- It is measured both ways: by direct data in the three sessions, and by the caregiver's report. Direct data come first; when the sessions gave no opportunity for the skill, the caregiver's report gives the baseline, attributed to the caregiver. When neither gives it, mark it [MISSING: ...]; never estimate it.

## Mastery criteria

- Most skills: 90%, as her goals usually end.
- Safety skills, personal information and skills for recognizing danger in the community: 95%.
- Crossing the street: 100%.

## Caregiver goals

- One set of goals for all the caregivers who take part, never one set per caregiver.
- Three standard goals: carrying out the interventions and environmental manipulations, teaching the replacement and acquisition goals, and using reinforcement.
- A fourth goal when the case needs it: a safety plan the caregivers carry out, or a skill that can only be taught at home or belongs to home, such as a toileting goal.
- Baselines start at 0%, since the caregivers have not been trained yet. When the sources show that the caregivers already apply some of the strategies on their own, because the child received services before and they were trained then, the baseline is 10% or 20%, by how much of that goal's strategies they already use; mark the choice [CHECK: ...] for her.
- Caregiver training is never by telehealth.
