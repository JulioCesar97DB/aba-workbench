# What the Florida payers look for

These are facts from the Florida Medicaid coverage policy and Molina Healthcare of Florida's authorization guide that bear on whether ABA hours are authorized. Every statement below comes from one of those two documents; the source is named in each heading. Use them to decide what to emphasize and what to flag as missing. Do not quote the policy or cite its section numbers in the assessment; the reviewer knows the policy.

## Who qualifies (Florida Medicaid policy 2.2)

Recipients under 21 who need medically necessary behavior analysis services to address behavior that impairs their ability to perform a major life activity. The policy recognizes functional impairment expressed through:

- Safety: aggression, self-injury, property destruction, elopement.
- Communication: problems with expressive or receptive language, poor understanding or use of nonverbal communication, stereotyped or repetitive language.
- Self-stimulating behavior: abnormal, inflexible or intense preoccupations.
- Self-care: difficulty recognizing risk or danger, grooming, eating, toileting.
- Other behaviors, including complexity of treatment, programming or environmental variables.

Our advice, not payer text: describing the child's impairment in these same terms helps the reviewer map the child onto the criteria.

The recipient must be referred by an independent physician or child psychologist, with a physician's order for behavior analysis and a Comprehensive Diagnostic Evaluation (CDE) led by a licensed practitioner. The CDE must include assessment findings and treatment recommendations for this child.

## Required standardized instruments (policy 4.2.1; Molina section 4)

- Vineland-3 Comprehensive Parent/Caregiver Interview Form for all recipients, plus the Maladaptive Behavior domain for ages 3 and older.
- BASC-3 Parenting Relationship Questionnaire (PRQ) for ages 2 through 18.
- The complete score reports, with outcome measure scores, are submitted with the authorization request.

Molina describes their role: the Vineland-3 and BASC-3 PRQ establish global adaptive functioning (communication, daily living, socialization), identify co-occurring emotional or behavioral concerns at a broad level, and support the medical necessity determination. They are not a functional behavior assessment: they do not capture behavior-specific, real-time data and are never used to build the FBA or the behavior plan. A CDE that happens to contain Vineland or BASC scores does not replace them.

## Why requests are returned or denied (Molina section 2)

Molina lists these under "CDE-related snags":

- Functional impairment described in only one setting, or not at all; impairment shown across home, school and community gives the complete clinical picture.
- Insufficient background and history: a CDE that reads as generic or template-based rather than individualized.
- Treatment recommendations not individualized to the member; overly brief reports.
- Diagnosis not stated explicitly by a clinician (scoring tools alone are not a diagnosis), not connected to DSM-5 criteria with examples, or without a severity level.
- No direct observation by the evaluating clinician; alternatives to behavior analysis not considered.

And under "authorization request snags":

- A behavior's function named without this member's data. The documentation must show how often the behavior occurs, at what rate or duration, and at what severity, with its impact. Function definitions without member-specific measurement do not establish severity or support the requested intensity.
- Parent or guardian participation not documented; if a parent cannot participate, this must be documented with an explanation and a plan to mitigate the impact.
- Other services not listed, or the request not explaining how behavior analysis complements rather than duplicates them.

## What makes the requested intensity credible (Molina section 5)

- The current level of functioning in adaptive behavior, communication, social skills, self-care and safety, informed by the Vineland-3 and BASC-3 scores.
- Scope: comprehensive treatment across several developmental domains typically requires greater intensity than focused treatment of specific skills or behaviors.
- Evidence of impairment across settings: school observations or teacher reports, the IEP, progress reports from other providers, the parents' accounts of home, community and social settings, and direct observation across settings and times of day.
- The member's actual availability (school hours, other therapies, family availability) and the caregivers' capacity to participate and carry out strategies.

## Other facts

- Behavior analysis services must reduce maladaptive behaviors and help the recipient reach the best possible functional level. The policy covers up to 40 hours per week of intervention (policy 4.2.2); for recipients under 21, services beyond that can be approved when medically necessary (policy, EPSDT paragraph).
- The parent or guardian should participate in treatment when possible and clinically appropriate; if they cannot, the reasons, the potential impact and how it is mitigated must be documented. Family adaptive behavior treatment guidance (caregiver training) is a covered service (policy 4.2.2).
- Seclusion and manual, mechanical or chemical restraint are not covered, nor are 1:1 aide, companion, chaperone, shadow or childcare services (policy 5.2).
- Discharge criteria must be objective and individualized; "when clinically appropriate" is not a criterion (Molina section 8).
