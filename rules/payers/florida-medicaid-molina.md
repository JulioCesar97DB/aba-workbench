# What the Florida payers look for

These are facts about Florida Medicaid and Molina Healthcare of Florida that bear on whether ABA hours are authorized. Use them to decide what to emphasize and what to flag as missing. Do not quote the policy or cite its section numbers in the assessment; the reviewer knows the policy. Write the child's case so that it plainly meets these criteria.

## Who qualifies (Florida Medicaid policy 2.2)

Recipients under 21 who need medically necessary behavior analysis services to address behavior that impairs their ability to perform a major life activity. The policy recognizes functional impairment expressed through:

- Safety: aggression, self-injury, property destruction, elopement.
- Communication: problems with expressive or receptive language, poor understanding or use of nonverbal communication, stereotyped or repetitive language.
- Self-stimulating behavior: abnormal, inflexible or intense preoccupations.
- Self-care: difficulty recognizing risk or danger, grooming, eating, toileting.
- Other behaviors, including complexity of treatment, programming or environmental variables.

Describing the child's impairment in these same terms (safety, communication, self-care, restricted and intense interests) helps the reviewer map the child onto the criteria.

The recipient must be referred by an independent physician or child psychologist, with a physician's order for behavior analysis and a Comprehensive Diagnostic Evaluation (CDE) led by a licensed practitioner. The CDE must include assessment findings and treatment recommendations for this child.

## Required standardized instruments (policy 4.2.1; Molina section 4)

- Vineland-3 Comprehensive Parent/Caregiver Interview Form for all recipients, plus the Maladaptive Behavior domain for ages 3 and older.
- BASC-3 Parenting Relationship Questionnaire (PRQ) for ages 2 through 18.
- The complete score reports, with outcome measure scores, are submitted with the authorization request.

Molina is explicit about their role: the Vineland-3 and BASC-3 PRQ establish global adaptive functioning (communication, daily living, socialization) and broad co-occurring concerns, and they support the medical necessity determination. They are not the functional behavior assessment: scores alone never establish the function, frequency or severity of a behavior. A CDE that happens to contain Vineland or BASC scores does not replace them.

## Why requests are returned or denied (Molina section 2)

- Functional impairment described in only one setting, or not at all. Impairment shown across home, school and community gives the complete clinical picture.
- Text that reads as generic or template-based; recommendations that are not individualized; reports too brief to show a thorough assessment of this child.
- A behavior's function named without this child's data. The documentation must show how often the behavior occurs, at what rate or duration, at what severity, and with what impact. Function definitions without member-specific measurement do not establish severity or support the requested intensity.
- CDE gaps: diagnosis not stated explicitly by the clinician, no link to DSM-5 criteria with examples from the child, no severity level, no direct observation by the evaluator, alternatives to behavior analysis not considered.
- Parent or guardian participation not documented, or other services not listed and coordinated. Behavior analysis must complement, not duplicate, school-based services, speech, occupational therapy, counseling and psychiatric care.

## What makes the requested intensity credible (Molina section 5)

- The current level of functioning in adaptive behavior, communication, social skills, self-care and safety, informed by the Vineland-3 and BASC-3 PRQ scores.
- Scope: comprehensive treatment across several developmental domains usually needs more intensity than focused treatment of a few behaviors or skills.
- Evidence of impairment across settings: school observations or teacher reports, the IEP, other providers' reports, the parents' accounts of home, community and social settings, and direct observation across settings and times of day.
- The child's actual availability (school hours, other therapies, family schedule) and the caregivers' capacity to participate and carry out strategies.

## Other facts that bear on this assessment

- Behavior analysis services must reduce maladaptive behavior and help the recipient reach the best possible functional level. The policy covers up to 40 hours per week of intervention; for recipients under 21, more can be approved when medically necessary.
- The parent or guardian is expected to participate in treatment when clinically appropriate; if they cannot, the reasons and how the impact will be mitigated must be documented. Caregiver training (family adaptive behavior treatment guidance) is a covered service.
- Seclusion and manual, mechanical or chemical restraint are not covered, nor are 1:1 aide, companion, shadow or childcare services.
- Discharge criteria must be objective and individualized; "when clinically appropriate" is not a criterion (Molina section 8).
- The BASC-3 PRQ measures the parent-child relationship: Attachment, Communication, Discipline Practices, Involvement, Parenting Confidence, Relational Frustration, and (ages 6 to 18) Satisfaction with School. Low T-scores on the first five scales and on Satisfaction with School, and high Relational Frustration, are the areas of concern. They are the natural evidence for caregiver training in the plan.
