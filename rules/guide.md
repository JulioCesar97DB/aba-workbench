# The assessment as a whole

## What it is for

The initial assessment has two jobs. It must persuade the payer's reviewer that ABA is medically necessary for this child, at the intensity requested, and it must give the team a plan the RBTs and the caregivers can carry out. Both depend on the same thing: every part of the assessment telling one story about one child, from the evidence to the plan.

## The clinical reasoning chain

The assessment is one argument, and each link rests on the one before it. Write every section knowing where it sits in the chain.

1. Sources. What the CDE, the IEP, the caregivers, the questionnaires, the score reports and the BCBA's observations say.
2. Deficits and behaviors. The areas where the child functions below same-age peers (scores, and their impact at home, at school and in the community), and the behaviors that interfere, each defined by what this child does, with its baseline.
3. Functions. For each behavior, the antecedents that set it off, the consequences that keep it going, and the function the evidence supports, written as what maintains the behavior.
4. Interventions. Preventive strategies chosen for each function from that function's antecedents; a functionally equivalent replacement skill for each function; one consequence procedure for each behavior or group of same-kind behaviors with the same function; teaching procedures for the skill deficits.
5. Goals. Each behavior and skill as a measurable goal (ABA Matrix builds the ladders).
6. Caregiver training. The caregivers learning the plan's strategies for their own routines.
7. Generalization and maintenance. The skills carried to this child's settings and people, and kept.
8. Discharge. Objective criteria for each behavior, skill and caregiver, and how services step down, without hours.
9. Recommendations. The services and hours she sets, justified by the scope of the plan and the child's availability. The hours are always hers.

A section that skips a link breaks the chain: a strategy with no function behind it, a goal for a behavior never defined, a deficit argued in Medical Necessity that no goal treats.

## The section map

Each fact has one home: the section that owns it states it in full. Other sections refer to it by the same name, in a clause, only when their own point needs it. Sections the AI does not write yet still own their facts; refer to them by name and do not write their content into the sections you draft.

ABA Matrix or the agency fills in some parts, and nobody drafts them: Documents Reviewed (checkboxes), the reason for referral, the Diagnosis Code table, the Intensity Keys (template text), the function matrix (checkboxes), the template blocks of the Crisis Plan (the risk-factor checklist, the caregiver requirements, suicidality and homicidality, the techniques to resolve a crisis), Consent to Treatment, and her signature line, which she writes. Never write their content into a section; refer to them by name only when a sentence needs it.

## What she repeats on purpose, and what never

Two things are carried on purpose through several sections. The family's priorities, set out in Background Information, return in Medical Necessity, Strengths and Weaknesses and Family/Caregiver Involvement, so the plan visibly answers what the family asked for; there they are referred to in a clause, never restated in Background's words. The household facts (who lives in the home, who cares for the child, who works) are Background's too; the caregivers' work hours, availability and willingness to take part in training are Family/Caregiver Involvement's. Each is stated once, in its home, and other sections refer to it in a clause. The safety response for dangerous behaviors, set in the interventions, is referred to in Risk Assessment and given in full in the Crisis Plan. Even these are referred to in a sentence where they are not owned; the evidence is not retold.

Nothing else is repeated: no score tables or runs of scores outside the section that owns them, no incident retold in section after section, no session note in every section.

## What must match everywhere

- The child's first name and pronouns, and how each person is referred to (caregivers by relation, professionals by role).
- The diagnosis, worded as the CDE states it.
- Every date that appears at all (narrative sections give few or none; the observation dates live in Assessments Conducted).
- Every score and its level, as the score report prints it.
- Each behavior's name, definition, baseline and function.
- Each replacement skill, acquisition target and procedure, by the same name.
- The settings, the other services and their providers, and the caregivers' availability and languages.

When two of her sections disagree on one of these, do not choose: follow the rules for her sections and mark it [CHECK: ...].
