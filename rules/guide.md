# The assessment as a whole

## What it is for

The initial assessment has two jobs. It must persuade the payer's reviewer that ABA is medically necessary for this child, at the intensity requested, and it must give the team a plan the RBTs and the caregivers can carry out. Both depend on the same thing: every part of the assessment telling one story about one child, from the evidence to the plan.

## The clinical reasoning chain

The assessment is one argument, and each link rests on the one before it. Write every section knowing where it sits in the chain.

1. Sources. What the CDE, the IEP, the caregivers, the questionnaires, the score reports and the BCBA's observations say.
2. Deficits and behaviors. The areas where the child functions below same-age peers (scores, and their impact at home, at school and in the community), and the behaviors that interfere, each defined by what this child does, with its baseline.
3. Functions. For each behavior, the antecedents that set it off, the consequences that keep it going, and the hypothesized function the evidence supports.
4. Interventions. Preventive strategies chosen for each function from that function's antecedents; a functionally equivalent replacement skill for each function; one consequence procedure for each behavior or group of same-kind behaviors with the same function; teaching procedures for the skill deficits.
5. Goals. Each behavior and skill as a measurable goal (ABA Matrix builds the ladders).
6. Caregiver training. The caregivers learning the plan's strategies for their own routines.
7. Generalization and maintenance. The skills carried to this child's settings and people, and kept.
8. Discharge. Objective criteria for each behavior, skill and caregiver, and how hours step down.
9. Recommendations. The services and hours the scope of the plan and the child's availability justify.

A section that skips a link breaks the chain: a strategy with no function behind it, a goal for a behavior never defined, a deficit argued in Medical Necessity that no goal treats.

## The section map

Each fact has one home: the section that owns it states it in full. Other sections refer to it by the same name, in a clause, only when their own point needs it. Sections the AI does not write yet still own their facts; refer to them by name and do not write their content into the sections you draft.

## What must match everywhere

- The child's first name and pronouns, and how each person is referred to.
- The diagnosis, worded as the CDE states it.
- Every date: evaluations, observations, documents, incidents.
- Every score and its level, as the score report prints it.
- Each behavior's name, definition, baseline and hypothesized function.
- Each replacement skill, acquisition target and procedure, by the same name.
- The settings, the other services and their providers, and the caregivers' availability and languages.

When two of her sections disagree on one of these, do not choose: follow the rules for her sections and mark it [CHECK: ...].
