# How she writes

The assessment should read as if she wrote it. These are the patterns her own texts show, and what her texts do on points she has not settled yet.

## Voice and person

- Professional, plain clinical English in the third person. Past tense for what happened during the assessment (what was observed, reported, scored); present tense for the child's current functioning.
- The child: the first name throughout, with the pronouns the sources use.
- Caregivers and other people: by their relation to the child ("her mother", "his grandmother", "the teacher") and, for professionals, by title and name as the sources give them on first mention ("Dr. ..., licensed psychologist"), then "Dr. ...". Her texts write "his mother reported that ...". Whether she ever uses a caregiver's own name is pending her answer; until then, do not.
- The BCBA is "the BCBA" in the third person, never "I" or "we".

## Attribution

Every fact names its source inside the sentence, and the kind of evidence shows:

- "According to the Comprehensive Diagnostic Evaluation completed by Dr. ... on ...,"
- "His mother reported that ..." / "During the parent interview on ..., her mother described ..."
- "During the home observation on ..., the BCBA observed ..."
- "The IEP documents ..." / "His teacher reported, in the IEP, that ..."

Name the source once per paragraph when a run of sentences comes from it; do not repeat "according to" in every sentence.

## The patterns she uses

- Score, then the child's life, then why it matters, then what ABA will do. Her Medical Necessity paragraphs run: the score or the report; a concrete incident from this child's day; why it limits independence, safety or participation; and "ABA will ..." naming the skill or the procedure.
- Strength, then qualification: "Although [name] can ..., [he/she] does not yet ... without ...". The qualification is a fact from the sources, never a guess.
- A closing that ties the evidence to the request: why individualized ABA is needed, in which settings and why there, the caregivers' part, and how progress will be measured.
- The intervention sections (not written by the AI yet) define first and then apply: "Name:" followed by one definition sentence, then the implementation with the child's name and concrete parameters.
- Generalization and Maintenance in exactly two paragraphs, as its rules give.

## Explaining numbers

- Every score, count or duration is followed by what it means: compared with same-age peers in plain words, or as what the child does or cannot do, where and with whom. A plain comparison helps the reader: "about 1 in 100 children his age would be expected to score this low or lower", when the report's figures support it.
- Give a number once, in the section that owns it. Another section that needs it names the behavior or the area and, at most, the one number its own point depends on.
- A sentence with three numbers or more and no clause saying what they mean is a list; rewrite it.

## Sentences she would not write

- Filler: "it is important to note", "it should be noted", "overall", "in conclusion".
- Hedging stacks ("may possibly suggest") and superlatives ("extremely", "very significant").
- Comments on the evidence or on the assessment itself, such as "These are school goals, not evidence that he has met them" or "Decisions will depend on whether gains hold, not on the dates of those changes". State the fact or the criterion.
- Sentences about the text ("This section describes ...") or addressed to her or to the reader.
- The same fact, incident or session note retold in several sections. It belongs to one section; others refer to it briefly, if at all.
- Lists in prose: a run of counts, scores or behaviors with nothing connecting them.

## Terms

- Diagnoses, instruments, scales and levels exactly as the sources and score reports write them.
- Behaviors by the names in her list of target behaviors, the same in every section. When the sources use two names for one behavior, use hers and keep it. Her preferred general terms (for example "elopement" or "leaving the area", "tantrum" or "verbal outburst") are pending her answer.
- Procedures by their standard behavior-analytic names (differential reinforcement of alternative behavior, functional communication training, and so on), spelled out on first use with the abbreviation after it when she uses one.

## Length

Only Medical Necessity has a length she set (in its rules). For the other sections, their rules give an interim length where the quality runs showed one was needed, pending her answer. Shorter is better when nothing is lost: a section is too long when it retells what another section owns.
