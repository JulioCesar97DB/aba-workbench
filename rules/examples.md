# Examples of the writing she expects

Each example below is about an invented child, Teo, 7 years old. Nothing in them is real, and none of their facts belong to the case you are writing: they show the shape and the way of explaining, not the content. Never reuse their sentences, names, dates or numbers, and use an example only for the section it belongs to.

## Medical Necessity: a deficit paragraph that explains

One paragraph from the middle of a Medical Necessity section, for the safety area:

> Teo's safety is the first priority his mother named, and the evidence shows why. She reported that he has run from her in parking lots 3 times since June, each time when she said it was time to leave somewhere he wanted to stay, and that he does not stop when she calls his name. During the community observation on 09/15/2026, the BCBA saw the same pattern: when his mother said it was time to leave the playground, Teo pulled his hand free and ran across the parking lot toward the swings, and he stopped only when his mother caught his arm at the curb. Running from an adult near traffic is the behavior most likely to cause him serious harm, and because he does not yet respond to his name or to "stop" when he is upset, his safety on every outing depends on an adult reaching him first. His mother's FAST responses (tangible 4 of 4) and both episodes point the same way: the running began when a preferred activity ended, which suggests that it is maintained by keeping access to it. Hitting his 4-year-old sister follows the same transitions at home (4 episodes in 2 hours of observation on 09/12/2026) and puts her at risk as well. ABA will teach Teo to stop when an adult says "stop", to stay beside his mother in parking lots, and to ask for more time instead of running, so that his safety no longer depends on someone catching him.

What makes it work: it opens with what is at stake and ties it to the family's priority; one concrete example carries the argument, reported and then observed; every number is followed by what it shows (the trigger, what keeps the behavior going, who is at risk); the questionnaire score appears once, where it supports the function; a second behavior is named in one sentence with its baseline; the closing names the skills ABA will teach for this area, without promising an outcome.

A weak version lists instead: "Teo engaged in 4 episodes of hitting, 2 episodes of property destruction and 1 episode of elopement during 4 hours of observation. FAST: tangible 4 of 4. MAS: tangible 4.8, escape 2.1." Every number is there and none of them means anything to the reviewer.

## Strengths and Weaknesses

A whole Strengths and Weaknesses section for the same child:

> Teo has strengths that treatment can build on. His mother reported that he dresses himself each morning without help and sets the table for dinner when asked; he can already carry out a familiar sequence on his own, which is a starting point for teaching the steps of his bathroom routine. During the home observation on 09/12/2026, he finished a 24-piece puzzle on his own in about 10 minutes and kept going after trying a wrong piece, so he can stay with a task he has chosen. He knows the name of every planet on the poster in his room, and his teacher reported in the IEP that space is the topic he most often uses to start a conversation with an adult; that interest can make practice motivating and give him a familiar topic for conversations with other children. On the Vineland-3, his Gross Motor skills were in the Adequate range. Although Teo follows two-step directions during play, he does not yet follow a direction that ends a preferred activity.
>
> His needs are in the areas argued under Medical Necessity. In daily living, his mother reported that he completes his bathroom routine only when an adult prompts each step. In communication, the BCBA observed that he speaks in full sentences but does not ask for help or for more time when he needs them. In flexibility, his mother and the BCBA both described his distress when a preferred activity ends. In safety, his mother reported that he runs from her in parking lots and hits his younger sister. With other children, his teacher reported in the IEP that he joins a game only when he has chosen it.
>
> To meet these needs, ABA will teach Teo to ask for help and for more time, to accept the end of a preferred activity after a warning, to stop when an adult says "stop", and to complete his bathroom routine step by step, using his ease with familiar routines and his interest in space to make the teaching his own.

What makes it work: each strength is concrete, sourced and tied to how treatment can use it; the qualification is a fact from the sources; the needs follow Medical Necessity's areas in its order and terms, one sourced sentence each, without its counts or incidents; the closing names skills, not strategies, and answers the needs just named.
