# Examples of the writing she expects

Each example below is about an invented child, Nadia, 5 years old. Nothing in them is real, and none of their facts belong to the case you are writing: they show the shape and the way of explaining, not the content. Never reuse their sentences, names, dates or numbers, and use an example only for the section it belongs to.

## Medical Necessity: a deficit paragraph that explains

One paragraph from the middle of a Medical Necessity section, for the safety area:

> Nadia's safety at home is the first concern her father named, and the evidence shows why. During the parent interview, he reported that she climbs onto the kitchen counter or the living-room bookshelf most days, usually to reach the art supplies kept on top of the refrigerator, and that in April she fell from the counter and bruised her hip. During direct observation in the home, Nadia was observed climbing the bookshelf 3 times in 2 hours; each climb began within a minute of her father saying that the markers were put away, and each ended when he lifted her down and handed her the markers. Because the bookshelf is not fixed to the wall, as her father reported, and she does not yet wait for an adult when something she wants is out of reach, a fall can happen whenever an adult leaves the room. Her climbing is maintained by access to preferred items: it began when an item she wanted was out of reach and ended when she got it. Her aunt reported the same climbing at her apartment, where Nadia spends two afternoons a week, so the risk is not limited to one home. Her other target behaviors, Tantrum and Property destruction, are argued in the paragraph on coping with change. ABA will teach Nadia to stay safely on the floor and to get what she wants without climbing, with skills matched to what climbing gets her, and her father and her aunt will be trained to support those skills in both homes.

What makes it work: it opens with what is at stake and ties it to the family's concern; one concrete example carries the argument, reported and then observed, with no dates and no quotes; every number is followed by what it shows (the trigger, what keeps the behavior going, who is at risk); the function is stated as what maintains the behavior, from the reported and observed episodes, with no FAST or MAS results (they belong to Assessments Conducted); the other target behaviors are named without their numbers; the closing states what Nadia needs to learn and the general approach, and names no procedure, strategy or program.

A weak version lists instead: "Nadia engaged in 3 episodes of climbing, 2 tantrums and 1 episode of property destruction during 4 hours of observation. FAST: social, attention or preferred items 4 of 4. MAS: tangible 4.6, escape 1.9." Every number is there and none of them means anything to the reviewer.

## Strengths and Weaknesses

A whole Strengths and Weaknesses section for the same child:

> Nadia has strengths that treatment can build on. During the parent interview, her father reported that she clears her plate after every meal and hangs up her coat when she comes home without being reminded; she already follows short routines on her own, which is a starting point for teaching the steps of getting ready in the morning. During direct observation in the home, Nadia named 14 insects in her picture book and pointed to each one when her father named it, so she learns and keeps new names quickly. Her aunt reported that insects are what Nadia talks about most and that she shows her insect stickers to every visitor; insect books and stickers can serve as reinforcers and give her something to share with other children. Her father's and her aunt's involvement will help carry out the plan's strategies and keep them the same in both homes. Although Nadia follows one-step directions during play, her father reported that she does not yet follow a direction to put an activity away.
>
> Her needs are in the areas argued under Medical Necessity. In daily living, her father reported that she puts on her shoes only after an adult places them in front of her. In communication, she was observed speaking in short phrases, yet she cried instead of asking her father to open a jar of paint. In coping with change, her aunt reported that Nadia stays in the car and refuses to go inside when a planned visit to the park is changed. In safety, her aunt reported that she climbs onto the windowsill in her apartment. With other children, her preschool teacher reported that she plays beside her classmates but does not join their games.
>
> What Nadia needs to learn follows from them: to dress with less help, to ask an adult for help, to accept a change of plans, to stay safely on the floor, and to join other children's play, building on her ease with short routines and her interest in insects.

What makes it work: each strength is concrete, sourced and tied to how treatment can use it, with no scores; her preferences appear as reinforcers and the family's involvement as a strength; the qualification is attributed to its source; the needs follow Medical Necessity's areas in its order and terms, one sourced sentence each, with examples other than Medical Necessity's and without its counts; the closing states what Nadia needs to learn, each the plain reverse of a need just named, and names no procedure, strategy or program.
