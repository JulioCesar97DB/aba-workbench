# Examples of the writing she expects

Each example below is about an invented child, Nadia, 5 years old. Nothing in them is real, and none of their facts belong to the case you are writing: they show the shape and the way of explaining, not the content. Never reuse their sentences, names, dates or numbers, and use an example only for the section it belongs to.

## Medical Necessity: a deficit paragraph that explains

One paragraph from the middle of a Medical Necessity section, for the safety area:

> Nadia's safety at home is the first concern her father named, and the evidence shows why. He reported that she climbs onto the kitchen counter or the bookshelf in the living room most days, usually to reach the art supplies kept on top of the refrigerator, and that in April she fell from the counter and bruised her hip. During the home observation on 05/14/2026, the BCBA saw her climb the bookshelf 3 times in 2 hours; each climb began within a minute of her father saying that the markers were put away, and each ended when he lifted her down and handed her the markers. Because, as her father reported, the bookshelf is not fixed to the wall, and she does not yet wait for an adult when something she wants is out of reach, a fall can happen whenever an adult leaves the room. Her father's FAST responses (social, attention or preferred items, 4 of 4) and the reported and observed episodes point the same way: climbing began when a preferred item was out of reach and ended when she got it, which suggests that it is maintained by access to that item. Her aunt reported the same climbing at her apartment, where Nadia spends two afternoons a week, so the risk is not limited to one home. Her other target behaviors, Tantrum and Property destruction, are argued in the paragraph on coping with change. ABA will teach Nadia to stay safely on the floor and to get what she wants without climbing, with skills matched to what climbing gets her, and her father and her aunt will be trained to support those skills in both homes.

What makes it work: it opens with what is at stake and ties it to the family's concern; one concrete example carries the argument, reported and then observed; every number is followed by what it shows (the trigger, what keeps the behavior going, who is at risk); the questionnaire score appears once, where it supports the function; the other target behaviors are named without their numbers; the closing states what Nadia needs to learn and the general approach, and names no procedure, strategy or program.

A weak version lists instead: "Nadia engaged in 3 episodes of climbing, 2 tantrums and 1 episode of property destruction during 4 hours of observation. FAST: social, attention or preferred items 4 of 4. MAS: tangible 4.6, escape 1.9." Every number is there and none of them means anything to the reviewer.

## Strengths and Weaknesses

A whole Strengths and Weaknesses section for the same child:

> Nadia has strengths that treatment can build on. Her father reported that she clears her plate after every meal and hangs up her coat when she comes home without being reminded; she already follows short routines on her own, which is a starting point for teaching the steps of getting ready in the morning. During the home observation on 05/14/2026, the BCBA saw her name 14 insects in her picture book and point to each one when her father named it, so she learns and keeps new names quickly. Her aunt reported that insects are what Nadia talks about most and that she shows her insect stickers to every visitor; that interest can make practice motivating and give her something to share with other children. On the Vineland-3, her Fine Motor skills were in the Adequate range. Her father reported that, although Nadia follows one-step directions during play, she does not yet follow a direction to put an activity away.
>
> Her needs are in the areas argued under Medical Necessity. In daily living, her father reported that she dresses only when an adult hands her each piece of clothing. In communication, the BCBA observed that she speaks in short phrases but does not ask an adult for help when something is out of reach. In coping with change, her father reported tantrums when an activity ends before she is ready. In safety, her father and her aunt both reported that she climbs furniture to reach things kept up high. With other children, her preschool teacher reported that she plays beside her classmates but does not join their games.
>
> What Nadia needs to learn follows from them: to dress with less help, to ask an adult for help when something is out of reach, to accept the end of an activity, to stay safely on the floor, and to join other children's play, building on her ease with short routines and her interest in insects.

What makes it work: each strength is concrete, sourced and tied to how treatment can use it; the qualification is attributed to its source; the needs follow Medical Necessity's areas in its order and terms, one sourced sentence each, without its counts or incidents; the closing states what Nadia needs to learn, each the plain reverse of a need just named, and names no procedure, strategy or program.
