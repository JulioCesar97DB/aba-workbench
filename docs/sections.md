# The report's sections

The section workspace: how the clinician starts, drafts, corrects, approves and copies each of the nine narrative sections of the initial assessment, how it is stored, and exactly what is sent to the AI. Decisions are the owner's (2026-11-22) unless marked otherwise.

## What she can do

Each section of the report (`src/shared/report-sections.ts`, in ABA Matrix's order) has a screen at `#/cases/<id>/report/<section id>` (`src/renderer/src/screens/Section.tsx`), matching the approved mockups (direction C, "Flujo 1" and "Flujo 2").

- **Start it** in one of two ways, shown as the two cards every step shares (`TwoWays`, owner's proposal §3.1). "A mano", "I already wrote it: paste or type it": she pastes what she already has in ABA Matrix, or types it; it is kept exactly as she wrote it (only line endings are made `\n`), used as context for the other sections and never contradicted. "Con la IA", "Have the AI draft it": from the case's sources, her other sections, her standing instructions and her session notes. Several sections she already wrote can also be pasted at once from the report's list, "Paste what you already wrote" (`#/cases/<id>/report/paste`, UX-6, [interface.md](interface.md#a-half-done-assessment-ux-6)): each filled box goes through the same save, one call per box, with the `pasted` flag: a pasted box is always saved as her own text (`hers`), also over an AI draft or her edit of one, so the other sections and the case map build on it (UX-6 review B1, the owner's decision); the earlier versions stay. With no AI connected the AI card keeps its color and its button reads "Connect the AI", to Settings (P7).
- **Watch it stream** as it is written, with Stop always in view. A stopped, refused, failed or cut-short answer is never saved: the screen says nothing was saved and her versions are unchanged. The screen and the file always agree (review round 4, R4-1): the Stop is checked right before the save and again in the case's save queue just before the write, so a Stop up to then writes nothing; once the write has started it finishes, and the screen shows that version and says it was saved because the text was already finished. A Stop (or "Skip the check") pressed while a request is still being prepared is remembered and sent as soon as it starts.
- **Correct it, both ways.** "Ask for changes in your own words" rewrites the whole text. Selecting a paragraph (click, or Enter/Space) opens "Change this paragraph": the model returns only that paragraph and the app puts it in place by character offsets, so everything outside that paragraph (the other paragraphs and the spaces and blank lines between them) stays exactly as it was. An answer that is not one paragraph (more than two paragraphs, another paragraph of the section repeated in it, or far longer than the paragraph) is not saved, and the screen says the AI returned more than the paragraph. "Edit by hand", on the left of the request box, lets her edit it. "Improve it with the AI" (on her own text) adds a version beside hers; her facts (times, dates, numbers, names, scores) stay exactly as she wrote them, and anything the model thinks is wrong becomes a `[CHECK: …]` beside her fact, never a silent change (real runs, 2026-11-23: an improvement had turned her 3:00–6:00 p.m. into 5:30).
- **Versions.** Every change is a new version. The version menu beside the title ("Version 2 of 2 ▾", owner's decision T3) lists each one with what made it, who wrote it, when, whether it is approved and, only for a Claude key, what it cost (P6: a ChatGPT plan spends no money per request); a muted line under the header says what the version shown is ("v2: “shorter, and mention the school” · today, 11:20 · see what changed"). Going back to a version only moves the current pointer; nothing is ever removed.
- **Approve** a version, then copy it (P2). Until it is approved, Approve is the primary action and Copy a secondary one beside it; once approved the chip says "Approved", Copy becomes the primary ("Copy for ABA Matrix") and "Remove the approval" moves into the version menu; once copied, Copy becomes "Copy again" (secondary) and the rail's "Next step" is the screen's one primary. A newer version takes the approval away until she approves again; the approval of an earlier one is kept in the history.
- **Copy for ABA Matrix**, in one click: see "Copy for ABA Matrix" below. The copy is marked in the case (T8), so "Copied" lasts: the chip says it whenever the approved version shown is the one she copied last, also after the case is opened again. Her own text, copied without approving it, keeps its chip "Yours" and offers "Copy again" (Approve stays the primary), as Review and copy does. The line's marker count comes from the copy's own answer (`markers`, `allMissing`), what the main process put on the clipboard. A line in place of the muted one says it was copied, how many `[MISSING]`/`[CHECK]` markers are still to find when she pastes, where to paste it in ABA Matrix (the section's title), that she has not approved it yet or that another version is the approved one, and links to the next section not ready; the chip says "Copied".
- **Lines that are not paragraphs.** Text pasted with single line breaks reads as one paragraph (many text boxes copy that way). The paste editor and the reading view say so and offer "Treat each line as a paragraph", which makes a new version; her text stays as she pasted it.
- **Her standing instructions** for each section (the rail), remembered for all her cases.
- **Her notes for today** ("Today's notes" at the foot of the case's sidebar opens them in a panel from the right), used in every request about this case until she presses "Start a new session", which archives them.
- **Checks** beside the text: structure, missing data and consistency with her sections and her own text (below).
- **A request that fails** says so in her language, "The AI could not finish and nothing was saved; your versions are unchanged.", followed by what to do for its kind of failure (owner's decision T11; `src/renderer/src/ai-errors.ts` maps each error code to a kind): sign in or connect again in Settings; a ChatGPT plan that was refused, where a paid plan "may be needed" (said as a possibility, never as fact) and another account or Claude is in Settings; check the internet connection; the AI busy, wait a few minutes; a usage limit, wait a while, check the account's usage or use the other AI; paste the text of some reports instead of their PDFs; choose the other model or AI; a request the provider refused, try the other AI if it happens again; check the cases folder; reopen the app when its writing rules could not be loaded; or try again in a moment. The provider's own words stay in English under "Technical details" (for OpenAI: could not be reached; the connection dropped while it was answering, nothing saved; an answer the app could not read, nothing saved; no answer in time; an error OpenAI sent while answering, with its code and words; a problem on its side, with the status). It offers "Try again", which sends the same request to the same AI, and "Open Settings" when the way out is there. A unit test reads the error tables (OpenAI's ChatGPT table included) and every code written in `src/lib` and `src/main`, and fails on a code without its kind.
- The sidebar's Report step counts the sections that are hers or approved ("3/9"); while she is in the report, the sidebar lists the sections under it, and the sidebar and the Report step show each section's state: not started (hollow dot), "Yours" (green), "v3" (amber: an AI draft waiting), "Approved" (green).
- **Which AI.** The connected provider; with both connected, a "Write with" choice, defaulting to the one she used last. The model is the most capable one unless she chooses the other in Settings ("Model", under the AI connection).
- **Not ready for real client data.** Until de-identification is plugged in, one slim line above every screen says so plainly ("Trial version. It does not hide the client's details before sending them to the AI yet."), and "What this means" opens the explanation ("Not ready for real client data yet").

## The screen

Owner's proposal §4 "4 · Informe", mockups 07, 08 and 09 (UX-3). The shared step header (eyebrow "Report · section 2 of 9", the title, one state chip, at most two actions with the primary last), and below it one of four states:

- **Not started:** the two cards; the rail: her instructions, which AI writes, "Next step".
- **The AI writing:** chip "The AI is writing", the only action Stop; an amber band ("Writing paragraph 4 · usually takes 1 or 2 minutes", that she can leave, that a Stop saves nothing); the text streaming in numbered paragraphs (a paragraph change shows the others as they are and the marked one being rewritten); the request box at the foot, waiting. The rail: what the AI reads (documents, her sections by name, her instructions, today's notes) and what happens when it finishes.
- **Written by hand:** chip "Yours · editing" ("Corrected by you · editing" when she edits an AI text), the save state ("All saved" / "Saving…") and Done in the header; the two-ways bar with "By hand" active; an editor that fills the pane and grows with the text, so the pane is the only scroll (T16), with the paragraph and word count under it; at the foot "Finished? Done keeps it as your version, ready to approve." and "Improve it with the AI". That button sends what she has on screen when she presses it: it is saved first as her version (this editing pass's), and that version is improved, never an earlier one. An AI version opened by hand and left untouched is not her text, so the button waits until she changes it (or until a pasted text has something in it); her own version, untouched, is improved as it is (`improveFromEditorVersion` in `src/renderer/src/section-view.ts`; UX-3 review round 1, S1). The rail runs the structure and missing-data checks on her text as saved; consistency "is checked when you finish".
- **A version to review:** the chip (AI draft, Yours, Corrected by you, Approved, Copied), the version menu, Copy and Approve (above). The text in numbered paragraphs; clicking one (or Enter/Space) marks it and opens "Change paragraph 2" under it. The request box docked at the foot of the pane (WorkArea's `footer`): "Edit by hand", the request in her words, "Create version N", and the examples under it. The rail in order: "Next step" first once the section is approved (then, after copying, its button is the screen's primary), "Before you approve" (each marker and structure point with a link to its paragraph, the consistency check, "You can approve anyway"), "Your instructions for this section", "Used" (the documents, behind "see"), which AI writes.

A failed request shows its sentence (above) with "Try again" only where sending it again can work (`aiErrorCanRetry`: the connection, the AI busy, a usage limit, a request the provider refused (one refusal is often not repeated; its sentence says to try the other AI if it is), the case's files, an unknown error) and "Open Settings" only where the way out is there and an AI is still connected (with none connected, the AI's own "Connect the AI" already leads there).

## Section states

What the other sections build on (`finalVersion` in `src/main/case-sections.ts`): the approved version; otherwise her newest final text: what she pasted (over an AI draft too: a pasted text is `hers`), her edit of it (`edited` based on her own text), or her hand fix of the version she had approved (`fromApproved`; the edit clears the approval as decided, but stays what the other sections use until she approves again), and her edits of those. An AI version she edited without having approved it is never context.

`sectionState` in `src/shared/sections.ts`: `not-started` (no version), `hers` (the current version is her own text, pasted or edited before any AI version), `drafted` (the current version was written by the AI, or edited after one), `approved` (she approved a version).

## Storage

### In the case's data file: `aba-case.json`, schema version 3

Version 4 adds the case map, an optional `caseMap` key: [case-map.md](case-map.md). Version 5 adds the copied marks (owner's decision T8): on a section, `copiedAt` and `copiedVersion`, when she last copied it for ABA Matrix and which version, both or neither (a file with one alone is damaged). They are written only by a copy, once the clipboard holds it, and change nothing else (not the version shown, not the approval) except the case's `updatedAt`, as any save does, so a copied case moves to the top of Home. A mark that cannot be saved is said at once on the screen that copied ("The app could not save that you copied it…", as a failed save is said) and the section does not show "Copied"; it is not kept with the failed saves, since saving it later would claim a copy that may not be what she pasted: copying again is the retry. `MIGRATIONS[4]` adds nothing: a version 4 file reads as nothing copied, and is written as version 5 on its next save. A section is "Copiada" (`copied` in its summary, `isCopied` in `src/shared/sections.ts`) while the version she copied last is the one she would copy now (`versionToCopy`: the approved one, or else the one shown); a newer version or another approval makes it not copied again, since ABA Matrix no longer holds what the app does. Everything else below is unchanged by versions 4 and 5.

`src/main/case-file.ts` and `src/main/case-sections.ts`. Version 3 adds two top-level keys; a version 2 file reads as version 3 with `"sections": {}` and is written as version 3 on its next save (`MIGRATIONS[2]`). Every rule of the data file still holds (lossless reading, unknown keys kept, a newer file never written to, durable writes, the 50 MB cap).

```json
{
  "schemaVersion": 3,
  "…": "the keys of version 2 (id, label, clientAge, times, files, sources)",
  "sessionNotes": { "text": "The mother confirmed today…", "startedAt": "…", "updatedAt": "…" },
  "sessionNotesArchive": [{ "text": "…", "startedAt": "…", "updatedAt": "…", "endedAt": "…" }],
  "sections": {
    "medical-necessity": {
      "currentVersion": 3,
      "approvedVersion": 3,
      "approvedAt": "2026-11-22T18:10:00.000Z",
      "copiedAt": "2026-11-22T18:12:00.000Z",
      "copiedVersion": 3,
      "versions": [
        {
          "number": 1,
          "kind": "draft",
          "createdAt": "2026-11-22T18:00:00.000Z",

            "requestedModel": "claude-fable-5-2",
            "servedModel": "claude-fable-5-2",

            "rulesVersions": { "assessment": "3", "requests": "1", "section-medical-necessity": "3", "…": "…" },
            "sourcesUsed": [{ "id": "…", "title": "CDE (2026-10-02)" }],
            "sectionsUsed": [{ "id": "background-information", "version": 1 }],
            "instructions": "Each area with its score…",
            "notes": "The mother confirmed today…",
            "deidentified": false,

    "background-information": {
      "currentVersion": 1,
      "approvedVersion": null,
      "versions": [{ "number": 1, "kind": "hers", "createdAt": "…", "updatedAt": "…", "text": "…exactly as she pasted it…" }]

```

- `kind`: `hers`, `draft`, `change`, `paragraph`, `improve`, `edited`. `hers` is a text she pasted or typed as a whole: a section's first text, and every box saved from "Paste what you already wrote" (`saveSectionText`'s `pasted` flag), which is `hers` over any earlier version, an AI draft included, with `basedOn` saying what it replaced. `edited` is her edit of the version that was current on the section's own screen. A kind or a section id a newer app writes is kept as it is (shown as an edit).
- Version numbers are never reused. AI versions never change. Her own text (`hers`, `edited`) is updated in place while she types in that same editing pass and it is still the newest version and not approved; any later pass, or an edit of an approved version, starts a new version (approved text never changes).
- **Nothing the app writes can make a case unreadable.** Every write of `aba-case.json` is first read back with the same rules it is read with (`readableCaseFileText` in `src/main/case-file.ts`); a change that would not read back whole is refused (`not-saved`, logged) and the file stays as it was. What a model returns is clamped to those limits before it is saved (a check keeps at most 20 items of at most 2,000 characters; model names at most 200); an answer longer than a section can be (100,000 characters) is not saved and says so. `instructions.json` follows the same rule.
- **What she types is never lost on a reload.** A section's text (with its editing pass), her instructions and her notes are saved through the main process's store of changes that could not be saved (`src/main/failed-saves.ts`), as the sources are: when a save fails, the change itself is kept there while the app runs, the section screen shows it (and Home lists the case) after a reload, and "Try again" saves what was typed. Closing or quitting asks first while any is left.
- **The kept text goes back in its box.** After a reload, the box it came from (the session notes, the section's instructions, or the section's editor, reopened in edit or paste mode) shows the newest kept text. A section's kept text that came from a box of "Pegar lo que ya escribiste" (each kept item says so, `pasted`, in the main process's memory only) also goes back in its box there, each time that screen opens and only while the main process still keeps it. The section's own editor shows it too, and saves it with the same flag on its first save, so it stays `hers` whichever screen saves it; whichever saves it first makes it go from the other, and a box whose kept text is gone empties, so it never puts an older text back (UX-6 review round 1, S1). A kept text from the section's own editor (an edit, its first save included) is that editor's alone: the paste screen never shows it, so an edit of an AI draft is never saved as `hers` (UX-6 review round 2, S1). Either way, the box shows the newest kept text instead of the older text on disk, while the alert still says it is not saved; "Try again" saves it, and her next keystroke builds on it (`useKeptText` in `src/renderer/src/autosave.tsx`). A box takes no typing until the page has read the list of kept texts (a few milliseconds at most; if the list cannot be read, it does not wait), so nothing she types can race a kept text it should show (review round 4, L-2). A box restores only once, when it opens; never later (the notes box opens with the case, in the "Today's notes" panel, which stays mounted while closed, so opening and closing the panel never opens a new box), and "Try again" never changes what a box shows, so nothing can put an older text back over one she has seen (review round 3, R3-1). In the main process, a failure of a box's own save counts as already shown in that box: typing and deleting during one outage stays one kept entry, her latest, and her next save that works clears it. After a reload no box shows anything until it says so (`failedSaveShown`; a reload resets it). A save that does not contain a kept text, from a box that has not shown it, never forgets it: a kept section text is first saved as its own version, and kept notes or instructions stay kept, with Try again. When a box shows the newest kept text, older ones kept beside it count as shown too.
- `generation` records what made each AI version: who answered, the rules versions, the sources, her sections, the instructions and notes as they were sent, whether the client's details were hidden (`deidentified`, false until de-identification is plugged in), the tokens and the cost (null with ChatGPT sign-in, which spends her plan).
- `consistency`: the check run right after an AI version is saved (below).
- About 10 KB per version: hundreds of versions stay far under the file's 50 MB cap.
- `sessionNotes` holds the session under way; "Start a new session" moves it to `sessionNotesArchive` (kept, never sent again). The key names avoid `notes`, which a version 2 file could hold as an unknown key.

### Her standing instructions: `instructions.json` in the app's data folder

`src/main/instructions.ts`, next to `settings.json` (`~/Library/Application Support/ABA Workbench` on macOS, `%APPDATA%\ABA Workbench` on Windows), written durably.

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "sections": {
    "medical-necessity": {
      "text": "Each area with its score, one example from home and one from school.",
      "updatedAt": "2026-11-22T18:00:00.000Z",
      "history": [{ "text": "an earlier text", "savedAt": "2026-11-21T10:00:00.000Z" }]
    }
  }
}
```

Nothing is lost: when she starts a new editing pass (more than 10 minutes after the last change), the earlier text joins `history`. A file this version cannot read whole (damaged, or from a newer app) is never written over: the instructions read as empty, saving is refused and the screen says it could not be saved.

### The model choice

`settings.json` gains `models` (`{ "anthropic": "claude-opus-5-5" }`, only when she chose one) and `lastProvider`. The choices are in `src/shared/models.ts`: Claude Fable 5.2 (default) or Claude Opus 5.5; GPT-6.2 Astra (default) or GPT-6.2 Sol.

## What is sent to the model

Every request about a case is prepared by **one function**, `prepareOutgoingRequest` in `src/main/outgoing.ts`, and nothing else can make one: `runSectionGeneration` takes only what it returns (a value tagged by that function), a unit test fails if any other file builds a section prompt or calls a provider, and `StreamRestorer` applies the request's `restore` to everything that streams back (holding back a `[` that is not closed yet, so a placeholder split across two pieces of the stream is restored whole). De-identification plugs in there: on every piece of text (sources, her sections, instructions, notes, the text being corrected and her request), with its restore map becoming `restore`. Until then, `deidentified` is false, `restore` returns the text as it is, and the interface shows the notice.

**PDFs** are sent as files (base64) for now. The owner decided PDFs are never sent to a provider as files; this is acceptable only while the app shows it is not ready for real client data, and the de-identification task replaces it with each PDF's text, extracted locally and de-identified in `prepareOutgoingRequest`.

Each request, in this order (`buildSectionPrompt` in `src/lib/rules.ts`):

| Part | What | Cached |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System | `rules/assessment.md`, `rules/guide.md`, `rules/style.md`, `rules/examples.md`, `rules/payers/florida-medicaid-molina.md`, `rules/instruments.md`, `rules/requests.md` (the system prefix alone: about 10,600 tokens since step 0 of the AI knowledge plan, 2026-11-23; about 3,000 before) | Yes: the same for every case and every request. |
| Sources | Every source of the case not removed, whole (never a prepared summary), each in a `<source>` tag named by its type, title and date | Yes: the same for every request of the case. |
| Task | The section's rules (`rules/sections/<id>.md`); `<her-sections>`: her other sections that are hers or approved, each labeled, final (unapproved AI drafts are never context); `<standing-instructions>`; `<session-notes>`; then the request: a draft; a change (`<current-text>` and `<her-request>`); a paragraph change (`<current-text>` with `<marked-paragraph>`, and `<her-request>`); an improvement (`<current-text>`, her facts kept exactly); a consistency check (`<current-text>`, and `<her-own-text>`: her own text of this section, when there is one) | No: it changes from one request to the next. |

Text she typed cannot open or close one of these tags (`quoteForPrompt`).

**The case map's draft** (step 1, pull request 2; [case-map.md](case-map.md)) is prepared in the same file (`prepareMapRequest`): the same system prefix and sources, then the map's rules and her content in the task, and a JSON schema for the answer.

**The evaluation's judge** (development only, `pnpm eval`; docs/evaluation.md) is the one other kind of request: `prepareJudgeRequest` in the same file builds it from the case's sources and its current sections, with the judge's own fixed instructions in place of the rules. Only a development build started with `--hidden` answers it, and nothing it does is saved to the case. It is the only request that asks for structured output (`outputFormat`).

**Caching.** Every section request also carries the approved case map after the sources (docs/case-map.md, "In section requests"), as that section gets it: it differs per section (D11 as changed on 2026-11-28), so the rules and the sources before it are the prefix every section of a case shares and reads from the cache. On Claude it is a third breakpoint, and with an OpenAI key a third explicit breakpoint (explicit mode writes up to four): a cache entry for that section's later requests (changes, improvements, checks), while another section still reads the rules and the sources at the second breakpoint. On the ChatGPT sign-in, where only the end of the latest user message is cached, a map message of its own would move that place per section, so the map goes in `instructions`, after the fixed first line and before the task, and the input stays the rules and the sources. The case map's own requests, the definitions review and the judge carry no block. Before the map: Claude: two breakpoints (after the system, after the sources) with the one-hour cache (`ttl: "1h"`, writing costs twice the input price instead of 1.25 times; she reads and corrects for longer than five minutes between requests). OpenAI with an API key: two explicit breakpoints (after the rules, after the sources), 30 minutes, as before. With ChatGPT sign-in the route refuses `prompt_cache_options` and `prompt_cache_breakpoint`, so only OpenAI's implicit caching applies, which writes at one place: the end of the latest user message. The task therefore goes in top-level `instructions` (GPT-6.2 appends them after the input, outside the cache), after a fixed first line (`CHATGPT_TASK_PREAMBLE` in `src/lib/providers/openai.ts`), and the input is only the rules (developer message) and the sources (user message): the same for every request of a case, so the first request writes them and every later one, checks and paragraph changes included, reads them. With the task as the last user message, as before, every request wrote a prefix no other request shared (real runs, 2026-11-23: 2,944 cached tokens, the rules alone, on 5 of 15 requests). `tests/sections.test.ts` checks that every kind of request (draft, paragraph change, change, improvement, their checks and a check run again) sends the same settings and input on this route.

**Whole-assessment rules keep their force on every provider** (review round 1 of the ChatGPT fixes, F3). OpenAI ranks top-level `instructions` above the developer message, so moving the task there would have put the section's task, her request, standing instructions, notes, her sections and the current text above `rules/assessment.md`, the payer rules and `rules/requests.md` on ChatGPT, while on Claude and with an OpenAI API key the task is a user message below them. The instructions therefore start with a fixed line: the rules in the developer message apply in full and take precedence over everything below; the text in `<her-request>`, `<standing-instructions>`, `<session-notes>`, `<her-sections>`, `<current-text>`, `<marked-paragraph>` and `<her-own-text>` is hers, to use within those rules, never instead of them ("to use" rather than "to act on", review round 2, R5: in a consistency check `<her-request>` is context for judging differences, not something to do). The line is the same on every request, so the cache win stays. Whether to re-read the quality of a few sections (a draft, a change, an improvement) on the real route with this layout before merging is the owner's decision, still open on 2026-11-23; no real request was made for this change.

**Caching on ChatGPT, measured** on the real route (2026-11-23, before the fixed line was added, GPT-6.2 Astra, effort high, an invented case of about 17,800 input tokens: rules 3,043, sources 11,378, the task about 3,300 as instructions): the input was identical on all four requests (a draft, its check, a paragraph change, its check), and the fourth read 13,952 tokens from the cache, the rules and all but 469 tokens of the sources (78% of its input); the first three read none. Cached states live on individual OpenAI machines and OpenAI routes each request (GPT-5.6 and later need no `prompt_cache_key` for that, by its guide), so not every request lands where the cache is. This route reports no cache writes (`cache_write_tokens` 0 throughout).

**A cache key per case on ChatGPT** (owner's decision, 2026-11-23). Since a request only reads the cache on the OpenAI machine that holds it, every request of a case on the ChatGPT route also sends `prompt_cache_key`, the same for every kind of request of that case (drafts, changes, paragraph changes, improvements, checks, checks run again) and different for each case, so OpenAI can route them together. The key goes to OpenAI, so it says nothing about the client: `src/main/cache-key.ts` makes it from a SHA-256 hash of the case's internal id (a random UUID the app made) with a fixed prefix, as "aba-" and 32 characters, never from the client's name, the case's folder name or anything she wrote. The route does not list the field among those it refuses. With an API key no key is sent (that route keeps its explicit breakpoints), and Claude has no such field. `tests/sections.test.ts` checks one key for every request of a case and another for a second case; `tests/openai.test.ts` checks it is sent only on the ChatGPT route. Its effect on the hit rate was not measured: no real request was made for it.

Claude keys its cache on the request's settings as well as its text (a change of effort invalidates it). So every section request runs with the same settings (owner's decision, 2026-11-23): effort high and 32,000 output tokens for drafts, changes, paragraph changes, improvements and consistency checks alike, with the same system and sources before the breakpoints. The first request of the hour writes the cache and every later request in the hour reads it, the checks and paragraph changes included. A unit test (`tests/sections.test.ts`, "M4") sends every kind of request through the real Claude request builder and checks that everything up to the last cache breakpoint is identical.

**Effort and output room** (`src/main/sections.ts`): every request at effort high with 32,000 output tokens (only what is written is billed). With ChatGPT sign-in the effort is sent as `reasoning.effort` and OpenAI echoes it back in the response (`"effort": "high"`, mode `standard`; real route, 2026-11-23), so it is honoured as a setting; GPT-6.2 reasons adaptively, though, and used no reasoning tokens on any of those four requests (14 of 15 in the first real runs). Each version and each check keeps its reasoning tokens (`usage.reasoningTokens`). The route takes no output limit (`max_output_tokens` is refused).

**Expected cost** (Claude with an API key; prices in `src/lib/pricing.ts`, checked 2026-11-21; a real case of about 30,000 tokens of sources, rules about 4,000, the task with her sections about 7,500, a draft about 8,000 output tokens with its thinking):

Since step 0 of the AI knowledge plan (2026-11-23) the cached system prefix is about 10,600 tokens instead of about 3,000 (the guide, the style guide and the examples joined it); with a section's own rules, sent in the task (about 400 to 1,900 tokens), the rules come to about 11,000 to 12,500 tokens instead of about 4,000: on Claude Fable 5.2 that adds about $0.12 to each hour's cache write and a fraction of a cent to every request that reads the cache; the estimate above has not been redone.

What changed with one effort for everything: the extra cache write per hour for medium-effort requests is gone (about $0.54 on Fable 5.2, $0.27 on Opus 5.5 each hour, $1.5 to $2.5 over an assessment on Fable), while each paragraph change and check now thinks at effort high and costs a little more (about +$0.05 and +$0.04 on Fable). Over a whole assessment the two roughly cancel: the estimate stays about $18 on Fable 5.2 and $9 on Opus 5.5. The first real runs should confirm it with `cache_read_input_tokens` on the checks and paragraph changes.

ChatGPT sign-in spends her ChatGPT plan, not money. The real cost of each version is recorded with it (`costUsd`) and shown beside its number.

## Checks beside the text

`src/shared/section-text.ts`, `src/main/sections.ts`.

- **Structure** (local, free): plain paragraphs only (no headings, lists, bold or tables, which ABA Matrix does not take), and as many paragraphs and words as the section's rules ask when they say so (`paragraphs: 7-10`, `words: 900-1250` in the rules file's front matter; since step 0 of the AI knowledge plan every section except the Crisis Plan has them, taken from her own assessments).
- **Missing data** (local, free): every `[MISSING: …]` and `[CHECK: …]` marker, with what it says; markers are highlighted in the text.
- **Consistency with her sections** (AI): after each new AI version, a second request (same provider, model and settings, reading the cache the draft wrote; see "Caching") checks it against her sections that are hers or approved, and against her own text of this section: the first version she wrote or edited by hand on the checked version's own lineage (each version's `basedOn`, back from the one checked; a hand edit on another branch of her versions does not count, review round 2, R2), her hand edit of an AI version included, since a fact she fixed by hand is her latest word on it (`ownTextBefore`; a predicate of its own, not the one that decides what the other sections build on, where her edit of an unapproved AI draft does not count). A draft, which comes from no text of hers, is compared with her latest hand-written version before it. A hand edit of an AI version adopts every fact in it, even one that version's check reported, if she fixed only a typo (review round 2, R3: the owner's rule, a hand edit being her word on the facts; the earlier finding stays on that AI version). A fact of hers that the version replaces with another value is a contradiction, with or without a `[CHECK: …]`; her fact kept as she wrote it with a `[CHECK: …]` quoting a source beside it (the right answer to "Improve") is not, nor is a fact of hers left out. For a change or a paragraph change, what she asked for since her text (the requests of the versions on that same walk back, oldest first) goes to the check in `<her-request>`: a difference she asked for is not a contradiction, and a value she asked for becomes her fact, so a later version that keeps her old value or gives a third one is a contradiction, unless a later request changed it again (review round 2, R1). The answer (`NO CONTRADICTIONS`, or one line per contradiction) is saved with the version, with its tokens and what it cost, as for a version (`usage`, `costUsd`; no cost with ChatGPT sign-in), and the version of her own text it compared (`ownTextCompared`). With nothing of hers to compare, nothing is sent ("Nothing to compare yet"). While only the check runs, the button says "Skip the check": the version is already saved. A check that failed, was skipped, or no longer holds because her sections changed since ("Your other sections changed since this check") can be run again with "Check again", which goes through the same preparation as every request (`checkSection`).
- At a narrow window, where the rail falls below the text, a line near the top ("Checks: 2 to look at") leads to them.
- **Cost.** With a Claude key, each version's line in the version menu shows what it cost, its checks included (every check run on the version added up), and the Report step what the AI cost for the case so far; with the ChatGPT plan no cost is shown (P6). A check run again replaces the result but keeps what the earlier checks of the version cost and used (`earlierCostUsd`, `earlierUsage`), so a failed or stopped "Check again" never takes money already spent out of the totals. Tokens are kept in the case's data file for versions and checks alike, and shown for neither.

## Copy for ABA Matrix

`abaMatrixClipboard` in `src/shared/section-text.ts`: the clipboard gets two forms at once (one `ClipboardItem`): plain text, its paragraphs separated by one blank line, and minimal HTML, one `<p>` per paragraph with no fonts, colors or styles (a line break inside a paragraph becomes `<br>`). A rich-text box takes the HTML and keeps clean paragraphs in its own font; a plain box takes the text. Once the clipboard holds it, `SectionService.copy` saves the copied mark (`markCopied`); a clipboard that fails marks nothing, and a mark that cannot be saved leaves the copy on the clipboard and says so. The same copy runs from the section's screen and from Revisión y copia (`screens/Review.tsx`). It has not been tried in ABA Matrix itself (no access from here): a one-minute paste into a field of an empty or invented ABA Matrix record would settle which form it uses.

## Writing rules for each section

`rules/sections/`. Medical Necessity has the BCBA's own structure. The other eight were written from the decisions file (`aba-assessment-template.md`): who reads it, what it covers, what to write, its gaps. Where the decisions file says too little to write a section's rules well, the file says so under "Not yet settled with the BCBA" instead of inventing clinical guidance (Background Information, Strengths and Weaknesses, Environmental Changes, Family/Caregiver Involvement, Discharge and Service Fading Plan, Risk Assessment, Crisis Plan). Generalization and Maintenance follows her two-paragraph description exactly. Crisis Plan gives no de-escalation techniques of its own (the decisions file does not give hers); Discharge tells the model not to describe the three phases. All are marked `confirmed: no` until she reviews them.

Step 0 of the AI knowledge plan (2026-11-23, from the audit of the quality runs and the owner's notes on how she writes in three of her assessments, used as patterns only, never as text) revised them without new app features. Each section now follows her order and length where her texts show them (Background Information's six topics, Medical Necessity's own direct observation paragraph, Strengths and Weaknesses closing on what the child needs to learn, Environmental Changes starting from how the child responds to change, Risk Assessment's four parts, the Crisis Plan scaled to the risk with her monitoring part); what she does the same in every case (her supports, how she trains caregivers, her discharge periods, how she monitors a plan) reaches the AI only through her standing instructions once she confirms it, and a choice for one child is a proposal; until then both stay `[MISSING: …]`. In detail: Medical Necessity argues the most impairing behaviors with an incident and what the numbers mean instead of listing every count and questionnaire score, and keeps the incidents when shortened; Strengths and Weaknesses names Medical Necessity's areas without repeating its numbers and qualifies a strength only with a sourced fact; Background Information says what it owns and leaves the behaviors' data, the scores and the current changes to their sections; Family/Caregiver Involvement names the procedures without describing them, states training delivery only when her standing instructions or a source give it, and has her usual length; Risk Assessment leaves every response to the Crisis Plan; Discharge states criteria in the payer's form and no sentences commenting on the evidence. The whole-assessment rules separate four kinds of content: facts; clinical reasoning, where "ABA will…" states only what the child needs to learn (the plain reverse of a documented deficit) and the general approach; her standard practice, written only as her standing instructions give it; and proposals (every named procedure, strategy, support, program title, parameter, crisis step, threshold, training delivery and hours), never written into prose until the case formulation exists and she accepts them there. Until then both of the last two stay `[MISSING: …]`.
